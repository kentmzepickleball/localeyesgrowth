A catering quote sent within about five minutes books at roughly 23 to 25 percent. The same quote sent the next day books at about 14 percent. That is nearly 40 percent of your revenue decided before you have said a single interesting thing about your service.

If you want one number for how fast to respond to leads, it is five minutes. Below is why that holds, what the delay costs in dollars, and the systems that make five minutes normal rather than your best day.

## How fast should you respond to leads?

Here is what the research across coffee carts and mobile bars shows.

| Response time | What happens |
|---|---|
| Under 5 minutes | Roughly 23 to 25 percent booking rate. About 70x more likely to close than the 12 to 24 hour window |
| Under 30 minutes | About 8x more likely to close than the 12 to 24 hour window |
| 12 to 24 hours | The baseline most businesses operate at |
| Next day or later | Roughly 14 percent booking rate. Nearly 40 percent of revenue given away |

Two independent lines of evidence agree. Mobile bar operators in our research cite the 70x and 8x multipliers, and quote-platform data across coffee-catering operators produces the 23 to 25 percent versus 14 percent split. Different segments, different methods, same conclusion.

Then the cleanest data point of all: The Knot and WeddingWire's own numbers, cited publicly by former Knot sales VP Alan Berg, show couples choose the first vendor who responds about half the time. Not the best vendor. The first one.

Flashquotes, whose coffee cart catering cost study covers 25,860 bookings from 578 operators, found operators who moved to instant quoting saw about a 23 percent lift in booking rate, driven by faster response rather than lower prices.

## Why the first responder usually wins

It is not that fast people are better at sales. Three things are happening.

**The buyer sent five inquiries, not one.** Especially on directories, where one form reaches multiple vendors. By the time you reply at 9am tomorrow, three people have answered, one has sent a quote, and the buyer has a frontrunner.

**Speed reads as reliability.** Your buyer is usually an office manager or event coordinator, and she is not evaluating your espresso. She is evaluating whether you will show up, be excellent, hit the budget, and make her look good in front of colleagues. A five-minute reply is the first evidence she has. A three-day silence is evidence too.

**Attention decays.** They submitted the form while the event was live in their head. An hour later they are in a meeting. A day later someone else solved the problem.

And almost nobody does this. The industry-standard autoresponder runs on a 24 to 48 hour cycle, so speed is still a real advantage, and it costs nothing to claim.

## What slow response actually costs

Run the arithmetic on your own numbers. Here it is using the Flashquotes median booking value of $1,040, against 100 annual inquiries.

- Five-minute response, 23 percent booking rate: 23 bookings, about $23,920.
- Next-day response, 14 percent booking rate: 14 bookings, about $14,560.

The gap is $9,360 on the same 100 leads, the same website, and the same prices. Nine bookings you already paid to generate and then gave away by being slow. That is where the "nearly 40 percent of revenue" figure comes from. It falls straight out of the booking-rate math.

Scale it. At a $1,500 average booking, which is what Valor Coffee, a public LocalEyes client, averaged across 158 bookings and $239,047 in twelve months, the same 100 leads produce a $13,500 gap.

Operators misjudge this constantly, spending months chasing more leads when the same effort on response time delivers a bigger number faster.

## Run this response-time audit this week

Almost no operator knows their median response time. Here is a five-day audit that costs nothing.

**Day 1: Pull the last 30 inquiries.** Inbox, form notifications, directory dashboards, phone. Record when each arrived and when a human replied. Not the autoresponder. A human.

**Day 2: Calculate three numbers.** Median response time (median, not average, because one four-day outlier hides a decent process), percentage answered inside five minutes, percentage that waited over four hours.

**Day 3: Segment by source and by hour.** Directory leads should be your fastest and are usually your slowest. Then split by time of day. Most operators find a dead zone, typically mid-event or after 5pm.

**Day 4: Mystery-shop yourself.** Have someone the business does not know inquire through your website form, your Google Business Profile, then by phone. Time all three. Note whether the reply was personalized, answered cost and availability, and ended with a question.

**Day 5: Calculate the gap.** Annual inquiries, times nine percentage points of booking rate (the 23 versus 14 spread), times your average booking value. That is your annual cost of being slow.

If your booking rate is under 15 percent, check response time before pricing. It is the likelier culprit and the cheaper fix.

## The systems that make five minutes normal

Speed is not a personality trait. It is four pieces of infrastructure.

**One person owns the inbox.** Not "whoever sees it." Name someone, and if that someone is you while you are behind a cart, that is the actual problem. Owners stuck serving routinely lose large inquiries because nobody was watching the phone.

**Notifications that interrupt.** Form submissions should hit a phone with a distinct sound, not a shared email checked at lunch. Route website forms, Google Business Profile messages, and directory leads into one place.

**Templates you can finish in 90 seconds.** Keep the first reply to one phone screen. No links, no PDF, no wall of packages. Mirror the prospect's tone, and end with a question rather than a period. Build four variants: corporate, wedding, directory, last-minute.

**Instant quoting for the two questions everyone asks.** Pricing comes up in roughly 80 percent of buyer conversations and availability in about 70 percent. An instant quote answers both at 11pm on a Sunday without you. It does not replace the call, it buys you the right to have one, the trade-off covered in [instant quoting vs the discovery call](/blog/instant-quote-vs-discovery-call).

Two smaller levers. Text rather than email, because email hits corporate spam filters. And put a visible phone number on the site, since operators in our research have closed corporate work within hours of adding one.

## What to say in the first five minutes

Speed without substance still loses. The first reply does four things and nothing else:

1. Confirm you are available on their date, or say plainly that you are not.
2. Give a starting-at range. Not a full itemized quote, not yet.
3. Show one proof point matched to their event type.
4. Ask one question and propose a specific time. "How about Monday at 2?" beats "let me know what works."

Then follow up until you get an explicit yes or no. Speed wins the first round, but plenty of these close on the eighth touch, which is the subject of [the follow-up cadence that stops leads from ghosting you](/blog/catering-follow-up-cadence).

## FAQ

### What is a good lead response time for a catering business?
Under five minutes during business hours, and under an hour outside them. Five minutes is where the booking-rate data breaks, at roughly 23 to 25 percent versus about 14 percent by the next day. If you cannot hit five minutes consistently, an automated instant quote plus a human follow-up inside 30 minutes captures most of the advantage.

### Should I call or text a new catering lead?
Text first, then call. Text lands, gets read, and does not trip the corporate spam filters that catch email, which matters because your buyer is often an office admin behind a strict gateway. Then call, because voice closes better than typing. Prospects who dislike calls get handled by email after a voicemail anyway, so calling rarely costs you.

### Does responding fast mean quoting a firm price immediately?
No, and you should not. Send a starting-at range that answers the cost question without locking you in before you know headcount, venue, and duration. The full proposal comes after the conversation. What kills the deal is silence on price, not the absence of a final invoice.

### How do I respond in five minutes when I am working an event?
You do not, which is why it needs to be someone else's job. Assign the inbox to a specific person, or use an instant quote tool plus a live answering service rather than voicemail. Owners who stay on the bar through every event reliably lose their largest inquiries, because the biggest opportunities do not wait for service to end.

### My booking rate is low. Is it pricing or speed?
Check speed first. A healthy quote-to-booking rate is 15 to 30 percent, and above that you are probably underpriced. Below 15 percent, response time is the more common cause and the far cheaper fix, so measure your median response time before changing a single number on your price list.
