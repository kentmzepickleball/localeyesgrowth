If your Google Business Profile is filed under "Coffee stand" instead of "Mobile caterer," you will not appear for "coffee catering near me" no matter how good your coffee is. That one setting is the most common reason a working mobile caterer gets nothing from Google. The other two reasons are an H1 that tells Google nothing about what you do or where, and a review count too low for a stranger to trust.

Those three account for almost every zero-lead audit we run. If you are a mobile caterer not showing up on Google, it is one of them, and none are about your food, your cart, or your service. All three are fixable this week.

## Fix 1: the category that is burying you

Your primary Google Business Profile category tells Google what kind of business you are, and Google uses it to decide which searches you are eligible to appear in at all.

"Coffee stand" says you are a fixed location selling drinks to people standing in front of you. So Google shows you to people physically nearby who want a cup of coffee, and hides you from the corporate planner three suburbs over searching "coffee catering" with a real budget. The same logic applies to a mobile bar filed as "Bartender" instead of a catering category, or a grazing operator whose profile never uses the word catering at all.

Change the primary category to Mobile caterer or the closest catering category, then add your other genuine services as secondary categories. It takes two minutes and it is the highest-leverage single setting in local SEO for a mobile operator. The full decision tree by business type is in the right GBP category for your business.

While you are in there, strip "LLC" from the business name. Nobody searches for your legal entity. Then leave the name, address, and phone alone, because editing those after verification can knock you back to unverified, trigger reverification or suspension, and take your rankings with it. Decide first, verify second.

The address itself is worth getting right. An address-verified profile beats a service-area listing in the map pack almost every time, and map-pack placement can drive around ten times the leads of the number one organic result. We had a suburb client stand up a profile inside the nearby major city and get leads within three days. Today effectively all their leads come from that city.

## Fix 2: the H1 that says nothing

Your H1, the largest line of text on your homepage, is the heaviest single on-page signal you control. Most caterers spend it on a feeling. "Where flavorful moments meet." "Crafted experiences, thoughtfully poured." "Elevating your event."

Google cannot categorize that, and neither can a human in the two seconds you get before they leave. Abstract hero copy is the most common website mistake in this industry.

The formula is [Service] Catering [City].

- Coffee Catering Los Angeles
- Mobile Bar Catering Atlanta
- Grazing Table Catering Phoenix

Underneath, add a subhead carrying the specifics: counties you cover, professional baristas or bartenders delivered to the venue, the recognizable brands you have served. That is where personality is useful. The H1 itself should be boring and correct.

Two refinements. Use the term with the traffic behind it, since buyers search "coffee catering" far more than "coffee cart." And make your meta title describe the service, not only your business name.

This is one line of text, and fixing it alone has moved a catering site from page three to page one. Before and after examples in the exact H1 formula that ranks.

Structure matters alongside it. Google ranks pages, not businesses, so one page covering weddings, corporate activations, cold brew, and four cities ranks well for none of them. After we redesigned Valor Coffee's catering page and added location pages, their inbound went from around one lead a day to around five within a couple of weeks.

## Fix 3: nobody has vouched for you

Around 90% of buyers look for social proof before they buy. A profile with two reviews does not read as a new business. It reads as a business nobody has hired.

Reviews are also a ranking input, so this is not only a conversion problem. A profile with ten more genuine Google reviews outranks a competitor sitting on a hundred low-quality directory listings. Given a free afternoon, collect reviews rather than submit to directories.

The volume math protects you too. Under 5 to 10 reviews, a single one-star costs you bookings. At 20 to 40 it still hurts badly. North of a few hundred it effectively disappears. Build that buffer before you need it.

What works for mobile operators:

- A QR stand at the cart or bar reading "having a good time? Leave a review," so every guest can review you, not just the person who signed the contract.
- The day-of ask from your admin, sent once you have confirmed the event went well. Not from staff mid-service.
- Friends, family, and early clients when you are starting from zero, to get past the point where the profile looks abandoned.

Then put the proof where it works. A dedicated testimonials page pulls under 1 to 2% of your traffic. Put your star rating and review count directly under the quote button on your homepage instead, where it drives the click, and scatter one-sentence snippets through the pages people read.

## FAQ

### Why is my mobile catering business not showing up on Google Maps?

Usually the category or the verification. If your primary category is Coffee stand, Bartender, or Restaurant rather than Mobile caterer, Google will not surface you for catering searches. If the profile is unverified, or you edited the name, address, or phone after verifying, it can be suppressed entirely. Check both before assuming it is competition.

### How long until I see leads after fixing my Google Business Profile?

Category corrections often show up within days. We have had a new profile in a target city produce leads inside three days. If the underlying website is also new, expect roughly 5 to 7 weeks of sandbox before organic rankings move, though the profile can start working well before the site does.

### Do I need a physical address to rank, or can I use a service area?

You can rank with a service-area profile, but address-verified profiles outrank service-area listings in the map pack almost every time, and the map pack can drive around ten times the leads of the top organic result. Whatever you choose, settle it before verification, since changing it afterward risks the profile.

### My site looks great. Why is it still not getting inquiries?

A good-looking site and a converting site are different things. Check the two-second test, load it on cell data rather than wifi, and confirm a high-contrast quote button is visible before any scrolling. Only around 1 to 5% of visitors will fill out a long form, so also give the rest a lower-commitment option like a menu and pricing brochure for an email.
